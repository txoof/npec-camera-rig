The first time for a calibration this reads the tags from its photo, about 15 seconds. After that the plots open at once.

Tag map: where tags read far from normal

Each tag is coloured by how far its size reads from the middle tag of this run. Look for a whole sheet in one colour.

{% call ui.help("About the tag map") %}

The table, straightened on the four corner tags, so a spot on the map is the same real spot on the table in every calibration run. Blue reads small, orange-red reads large, pale is normal. Tags more than 1.5% off are also outlined in black and marked − or +. Grey means no tag: nothing is guessed between tags.

A whole sheet in one colour usually means that sheet printed at the wrong size, so measure it with callipers. Colour spread over the whole table, rather than one sheet, points at the camera instead. The histogram under the map counts the tags at each distance from the median, so one stray tag can be told from a group of them.

The colour range (2%) and the outline line (1.5%) are fixed choices, not measurements, so a good run stays pale. The scale is fitted to these same tags, so a whole bad sheet pulls the fit toward itself and reads somewhat less far off than it really is.

The map's proportions are those of the corner tags in the photo. The camera looks straight down, so they are close to the real table, but they are not measured. This is for setup only. How far a measurement can be trusted comes from validation.

Radial plot: is there lens distortion?

Each dot is one tag, by its distance from the centre of the photo. A steady climb or fall toward the right would mean lens distortion.

{% call ui.help("About the radial plot") %}

Across: a tag's distance from the centre of the photo, in pixels. Up and down: how far its size reads from the fitted scale, in percent, with its sign kept. The line is a straight fit through the dots.

Dots that climb or fall steadily toward the right (the frame corners) would mean lens distortion. This is the one measurement that would justify modelling the lens (CLAUDE.md). A cloud with no trend means no lens model is needed. The known low reading near the centre of the frame is a dip at the left end, not a trend toward the corners, and does not count.

R² says how much of the up-and-down spread the straight line explains, from 0 (none) to 1 (all of it). It ignores size: read the slope, in percent per pixel, with it. It only sees straight trends, so read the dots too.
