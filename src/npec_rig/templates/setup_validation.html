What validation shows, and how to do a round

Calibration alone cannot show how accurate area measurements are in each tray slot, because it is checked against the same tags it was fitted to. Validation moves the tag sheets to different trays and new positions, then measures every tag again. When a slot gives similar results across several rounds, its error belongs to that slot, not to a particular sheet, and the slot's ±% figure is what a new object measured there should expect.

Each round: leave the trays where they are. Put each sheet on a different tray, 2 to 5 cm from where a sheet lay before, one sheet per tray. Lay each sheet inside the tray rim, not over it. Press it down along all four edges. Check that all four corners touch the tray and nothing is under the sheet. Step out of view, close the chamber door, tick "The sheets are pressed flat and the chamber door is closed", then press Capture round. 3 counted rounds complete a validation; more rounds only add tags.

Rounds spread over a longer time give more certainty, because conditions in the chamber vary: readings rise and fall on a cycle of about 19 minutes, and an open door changes the air and light. So close the door before every round, take the rounds at least 10 minutes apart and at least 20 minutes from first to last; the countdown next to Capture round shows when the next round is due. A rushed validation still counts, but says so.
