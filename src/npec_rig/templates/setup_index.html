Not recommended.") }} {{ ui.switch("auto-exposure", "Auto exposure", "Off: the camera no longer adjusts to the light, and the rig cannot tell photos taken with the lights off. Keep it on during an experiment.") }} {% call ui.help("About Auto exposure") %}

Auto exposure lets the camera set exposure and gain for each photo as the light changes. The rig tells a photo taken with the lights off from how the camera's auto exposure reacts to the light. With Auto exposure off, the camera reports the same state for lit and dark photos (metrology log 29.4), so the rig cannot tell them apart. The Gallery marks each such photo "Lights not checked: auto exposure was off". Its plant areas are still counted, dark photos too. Keep Auto exposure on during an experiment unless you need fixed settings.

Exposure is how long the sensor collects light for one photo. Each press is one third of a camera stop, shown as a shutter speed such as 1/125 s, or tap the value to type milliseconds.

Gain brightens the image in the sensor, like a camera's ISO. 1 is the sensor's own sensitivity. More gain means more noise, so prefer a longer exposure.
