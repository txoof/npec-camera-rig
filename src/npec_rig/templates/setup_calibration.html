Step 1 - Place calibration and corner sheets

Lay a calibration sheet on every tray, plus the corner/reference sheets at all four corners of the table.

Blue = a calibration sheet on that tray, every tray. Amber dots = the four corner/reference sheets, one per table corner (N/S/E/W, top-down view: top is north, right is east).

Step 3 - Check squareness and coplanarity

Check that the camera is not rotated, tilted, or angled unevenly across the table.

More about this step

Physically adjust the camera until the East side of the table (opposite the drain) is in view. Drag the magenta guide-line tabs at the frame's edge onto known perpendicular lines in the scene (e.g. the table's own edges), if they line up, the camera is not rotated or badly tilted. A straightening aid only; dragged positions are not saved and reset on reload.

Colored rings on the corner tags show how co-planar the table is with the camera, live (no zero step needed, works as soon as 2+ corner tags are in frame). Each ring's percentage is that tag's apparent size compared to the average of the others: + means it looks bigger, i.e. closer to the camera than the rest; − means smaller, i.e. farther away. Green is within 3% (co-planar); amber is 3 to 8% (nudge that corner); red is over 8% (that corner needs real adjustment, not a small nudge). Nudge the rig until every ring is green. The magenta guide lines are a separate, unrelated straightening aid -- they never turn red, amber, or green, so they cannot be mistaken for the rings' verdict.

Step 5 - Read the report

The result above states the scale, coverage, and any coverage gaps found. If it says coverage is weak somewhere, open Diagnostics below to see exactly where, add a sheet there, and run calibration again. There is no need to redo Steps 1 to 3 unless something actually moved.

The first time for a calibration, this reads the tags from its saved photo, which can take about 15 seconds. After that the plots open from storage. Use this to see the active calibration's diagnostics without running a new one (e.g. after promoting one from history below).

Diagnostics

Tag map: where tags read far from normal

The table, straightened on the four corner tags. Each calibration tag is filled with how far its size reads from the middle (median) tag of this run: blue reads small, orange-red reads large, pale is normal. Tags more than 1.5% off are also outlined in black. A whole sheet in one colour usually means that sheet printed at the wrong size: measure it with callipers. The histogram under the map counts the tags, so one stray tag can be told from a group. This is for setup only. How far a measurement can be trusted comes from validation.

Table size for the tag map

Press "Save" any time. No camera capture is needed, just these three numbers. They only affect whether the tag map is drawn to scale, nothing else, so there is no need to run a calibration again to fix one of them.

Radial plot: is there lens distortion?

Each dot is one calibration tag: across, its distance from the centre of the photo; up and down, how far its size reads from the fitted scale, with its sign. Dots that climb or fall steadily toward the right (the frame corners) would mean lens distortion, the one finding that justifies modelling the lens (CLAUDE.md). A cloud with no trend means none is needed. The known low reading near the centre is a dip at the left end and does not count. R² says how much of the spread a straight line explains, from 0 to 1; read it with the slope and the dots, not alone.

Step 6 - Validate

Leave the calibration and corner sheets where they are. Validation moves each sheet to a different tray and measures again, 3 rounds, to show how far an area can be trusted in each tray slot. Close the chamber door before every round, and spread the rounds over at least 20 minutes. Remove the sheets after validation, before starting a growth run.
